## The core idea

Null turns the total return swap into a pooled onchain market. The Receiver pool takes the return of an oracle-tracked asset; the Payer pool supplies the collateral that backs that exposure. Each side is represented by fungible ERC-4626 shares.

A market is uniquely identified by three immutable dependencies:

```text
(collateral, oracle, frm)
```

- `collateral` is the ERC-20 deposited into both pools.
- `oracle` supplies the tracked asset price.
- `frm` is the funding-rate model.

The market redistributes collateral it holds and attributes to the two pools. It does not lend, create debt, use liquidations, or create an undercollateralized receivable.

## Receiver

Receiver is the synthetic-long side. A user deposits collateral, receives Receiver pool shares, and owns a proportional claim on that pool. For an ordinary settled price move:

- a price increase moves value from Payer to Receiver;
- a price decrease moves value from Receiver to Payer.

Funding can move in either direction. A positive signed funding rate means Receiver pays Payer; a negative rate means Payer pays Receiver.

## Payer

Payer supplies the reserve collateral backing Receiver gains and takes the opposite price exposure. A Payer can keep that directional exposure or hedge it externally. Null defines the onchain payoff but does not execute or maintain an external hedge.

## Example

Ignore funding for the moment. Suppose the Receiver pool is 1,000 collateral units, the Payer pool is 200, and the oracle price falls by 2%:

```text
Price PnL = 2% * 1,000 = 20

Receiver: 1,000 -> 980
Payer:      200 -> 220
```

Receiver loses 2%, while Payer gains 10% because the same 20-unit transfer is measured against a smaller pool. In a real update, funding and price PnL are calculated from the prepared pool state, netted, and then applied as one solvency-bounded transfer.

## Shares are pooled claims

Each share is a proportional claim on one pool's current assets, not a bilateral position with a private entry price. If you own 10% of a pool and no shares are minted or burned, you continue to own 10% after settlement; the value of that claim changes with the pool.

## How value moves

Each settlement follows this order:

1. Read and validate the oracle price.
2. Reconcile the market's external collateral balance.
3. Charge the annualized collateral fee.
4. Query the FRM against that prepared state.
5. Net funding with price PnL and cap the debit at `max(pool assets - MIN_POOL_ASSETS, 0)`.
6. Store the new timestamp and price.

If the price circuit breaker fires, the update skips price PnL but still accrues funding and stores the new price as the next anchor.

## Why full collateralization matters

The net funding-and-PnL transfer cannot debit a pool below `MIN_POOL_ASSETS` if it starts above that floor. Reconciliation, collateral fees, and user withdrawals are separate and can leave a pool below the floor. The accounting invariant is:

```text
receiverPool + payerPool + protocolFees == collateral.balanceOf(market)
```

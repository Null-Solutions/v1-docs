Null turns a total return swap into a pooled onchain market. The **Receiver** pool receives the return of an oracle-tracked asset. The **Payer** pool supplies the reserve that backs that exposure and takes the opposite price transfer. Each pool issues fungible ERC-4626 shares.

A market is uniquely identified by three immutable dependencies:

```text
(collateral, oracle, frm)
```

- `collateral`: ERC-20 deposited into both pools (often a Morpho Vault V2 share)
- `oracle`: tracked asset price (`1e36` scale)
- `frm`: funding-rate model address queried at settlement

The market redistributes collateral it already holds between the two pools. Spot does not lend the tracked asset, create Null-native debt, or liquidate users. Leveraged Morpho loops are separate periphery; see [Morpho](/learn/morpho).

## Receiver

Receiver is the synthetic-long side. You deposit collateral, receive Receiver shares, and own a proportional claim on that pool.

For an ordinary settled price move:

- price **up** → value moves Payer → Receiver
- price **down** → value moves Receiver → Payer

**Funding** is paid from collateral APR: Receivers pay vault yield on Receiver notional to Payers. See [Funding and carry](/learn/funding-and-carry).

Receiver is synthetic exposure, not ownership of the tracked asset.

## Payer

Payer supplies the reserve that keeps Receiver fully collateralized. Economically, a Payer is a **negative-size position in the Receiver pool**, with the market's collateral held inside that structure. When the Payer pool is smaller than Receiver, the same collateral transfer is a larger fraction of Payer assets, so Payer share value moves more in percentage terms.

Payers receive funding from Receiver-side vault yield and also earn collateral yield on their own shares. A Payer can keep directional exposure or hedge externally.

## Simplified price-only example

This example ignores funding, fees, integer rounding, and the pool floor. Receiver has 1,000 collateral units, Payer has 200, and the tracked price falls 2%:

```text
Price PnL = 2% × 1,000 = 20

Receiver: 1,000 → 980   (−2%)
Payer:      200 → 220   (+10%)
```

In a real update, the market first reconciles collateral and charges the collateral fee. It then calculates funding and price PnL from that prepared state, nets them, and applies one solvency-bounded transfer. See [Settlement](/developers/settlement).

## Shares are pooled claims

Each share is a percentage of one pool's current assets, not a bilateral position with a private entry price. If you own 10% of a pool and no shares mint or burn, you still own 10% after settlement.

The app labels Spot enter/exit as **Buy** / **Sell**. Onchain these are ERC-4626 deposit / redeem.

## How value moves

Each settlement:

1. Read and validate the oracle price
2. Reconcile external collateral balance
3. Charge the annualized collateral fee
4. Query the FRM against prepared state
5. Net funding with price PnL
6. Cap the paying pool's debit at `max(pool assets − MIN_POOL_ASSETS, 0)` and transfer it
7. Store the new timestamp and `1e36`-scaled price

If the [circuit breaker](/security/circuit-breaker) fires, price PnL is skipped; funding still accrues and the new price becomes the next anchor.

## Why full collateralization matters

The net funding-and-PnL transfer cannot debit a pool below `MIN_POOL_ASSETS` if it starts above that floor. Reconciliation, collateral fees, and withdrawals are separate and can leave a pool below the floor. The accounting invariant is:

```text
receiverPool + payerPool + protocolFees == collateral.balanceOf(market)
```

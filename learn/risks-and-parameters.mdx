Null V1 is fully collateralized, but every position can lose value. Full collateralization bounds what settlement can transfer; it does not remove directional, reserve, oracle, collateral, smart-contract, liquidity, funding, or parameter risk. There is no liquidation. Your shares remain a proportional claim on whatever assets remain in their pool.

## Directional and reserve risk

Receiver gains from an ordinary settled price increase and loses from a decrease. Signed funding can add to or offset that return. With the standard script parameters, Receiver pays funding when Payer coverage is below `P = 0.5R` and receives it above that target.

Payer takes the opposite price transfer. The whole Payer pool backs Receiver exposure notional `R`; each holder owns its pro-rata share of that notional. When the Payer pool is small, sustained price increases can therefore consume a larger fraction of its assets. Moves inside the circuit-breaker threshold settle in full. Thin Payer coverage also caps how much Receiver can collect from an increase.

There is no liquidity backstop. A redemption pays the pool's current assets according to ERC-4626 share accounting, not a guaranteed principal amount.

## Solvency and pool floors

Funding and price PnL are netted before collateral moves. The debit from either pool is limited to:

```text
settleable assets = max(pool assets - MIN_POOL_ASSETS, 0)
```

`MIN_POOL_ASSETS` is `1e6` raw collateral units, so its human-readable value depends on collateral decimals. The floor applies only when the net funding-and-price-PnL leg debits a pool, and it applies regardless of share supply. If a prepared pool starts above the floor, that transfer cannot take it below the floor. If it is already at or below the floor, its settleable amount is zero.

Reconciliation losses, collateral fees, and user withdrawals do not use this cap and can leave a pool below the floor or at zero. Any funding or price-PnL liability beyond the settleable amount is not recorded as debt or recovered later. The floor prevents settlement from wiping a live pool to zero; it does not guarantee the economic value of a share.

## Oracle risk

Each market has one immutable oracle address. Settlement calls `oracle.price()`, requires a nonzero result, and expects prices normalized to `1e36`. A revert or zero price blocks the pool action that triggered settlement.

Oracle delays, stale escape paths, bad reports, basis differences, or a mismatch between the tracked asset and the displayed market can produce unexpected settlement. Integrators should expose the oracle address and implementation rather than relying only on a ticker.

Some updatable oracle wrappers include a stale-price escape path. During an ordinary stale period, price reads revert and market actions that need a valid price cannot proceed. After a prolonged stale period, currently 10 days in the Pyth and Chainlink wrappers, the oracle can return the last stored price so users are not permanently locked by a retired or unavailable feed.

That escape path restores liveness; it does not guarantee current fair value. You bear the basis risk between the last available oracle price and the asset's current value. See [Oracles](/markets/oracles) for implementation-specific behavior.

## Circuit breaker

The circuit breaker compares the absolute price change with a time-scaled limit:

```text
allowed delta = previous price * 700 * elapsed / (10,000 * 1 hour)
```

If the move is larger, settlement skips price PnL for that update. It does not revert: signed funding still accrues, and the new oracle price is stored as the next anchor. The skipped amount is neither clamped nor deferred. This limits a single abnormal update but does not protect against gradual moves that remain inside the bound or repeated resets from successively higher anchors. See [Circuit breaker](/security/circuit-breaker).

## Collateral risk

The market assumes a standard ERC-20 whose sender and receiver balances change by exactly the transferred amount. Fee-on-transfer tokens are unsupported. Pauses, blacklists, rebases, and losses in an external vault can still affect availability or value.

Before funding and price PnL, settlement reconciles actual collateral against:

```text
receiverPool + payerPool + protocolFees
```

After reserving protocol fees, external gains or losses are distributed pro rata across the pools. Reconciliation keeps the accounting aligned with token balances; it does not remove collateral risk. See [Collateral](/markets/collateral).

## Empty or one-sided markets

If either pool is empty, settlement applies neither funding nor price PnL. It still prepares the state and stores the latest timestamp and price. Two-sided exposure starts only after both pools have assets.

## Smart-contract and integration risk

Core accounting spans the Factory, Market, Pool, and `SettlementLib`; routers and Lens are periphery. Controls include duplicate-market prevention, market callbacks restricted to its pools, router deadlines and slippage checks, and reentrancy guards. These controls and any audit do not guarantee that the contracts are bug-free.

Direct ERC-4626 previews use the last settled state and can diverge from execution because the market settles first. Use settlement-aware Lens projections where possible, enforce slippage and deadlines, and verify contract addresses.

## Fees

Each market starts with zero entry, exit, and annualized collateral fees. The factory owner can change them through `setFees`, and immutable constants cap each value at 500 basis points. A fee change settles the market before storing the new values. Fees accrue in `protocolFees` and can be swept only to the factory owner. See [Fees](/developers/fees).

Deposits and redeems use fee-on-total accounting. Mints and withdrawals use fee-on-raw accounting. Integrators should rely on ERC-4626 preview functions for estimates, then apply slippage controls for execution because the market updates before the action completes.

## Immutable market parameters

Each market is unique by:

```text
(collateral, oracle, frm)
```

The dependencies cannot be changed after creation. The factory owner must enable an FRM before anyone can create a market with it, and enabled FRMs cannot be disabled.

For the standard imbalance FRM:

```text
f = mu * (cR - P) / (cR + P)
```

`c` controls the zero-funding coverage target, while `mu` controls sensitivity and bounds `|f|`. The standard deployment script uses `c = 0.5` and `mu = 1`, but these values belong to that FRM deployment, not to the market contract or every possible FRM. Selecting another enabled FRM can change the parameters or funding behavior.

## Evaluation checklist

Before using a market, check:

- collateral address and token behavior;
- oracle address, price source, and liveness policy;
- FRM address, formula, `c`, and `mu`;
- Receiver and Payer liquidity;
- current signed funding rate and the Receiver exposure notional allocated to Payer shares;
- entry, exit, and collateral fees;
- circuit-breaker behavior and the `MIN_POOL_ASSETS` floor;
- contract addresses, liquidity depth, and execution slippage.

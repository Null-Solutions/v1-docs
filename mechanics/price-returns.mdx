Price returns are the mechanism by which Null V1 converts real-world asset price movements into gains and losses for pool participants. Receivers gain when the reference price rises and lose when it falls; payers take the opposite exposure. Crucially, no position can be liquidated — losses are bounded by the capital each side has deposited, not by any margin requirement.

## How Price-Based PnL Is Calculated

When the oracle reports a new price, the protocol first computes a **receiver notional** — the effective size of the receiver position in price units — and then scales the price delta by that notional to arrive at a dollar transfer between pools.

```
receiverNotional = receiverPool * PRICE_SCALE / lastPrice
receiverPnl      = priceDelta  * receiverNotional / PRICE_SCALE
```

Where `priceDelta = currentPrice - lastPrice`.

<Info>
  `PRICE_SCALE` is a fixed constant used for precision. All price values from the oracle are expected to be denominated in the same scale. The net effect is that `receiverPnl ≈ receiverPool * (priceDelta / lastPrice)` — i.e. the percentage price change applied to the full receiver pool.
</Info>

**Direction of transfer:**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Price rises" icon="arrow-trend-up">
    `priceDelta > 0` → `receiverPnl > 0`. Capital moves from the payer pool to the receiver pool.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Price falls" icon="arrow-trend-down">
    `priceDelta < 0` → `receiverPnl < 0`. Capital moves from the receiver pool to the payer pool.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The 7% Per-Hour Circuit Breaker

To protect against oracle manipulation, stale prices, or extreme short-term volatility, the protocol enforces a maximum price change of **7% (700 basis points) per one-hour window**.

```
maxChange = lastPrice * 700 / 10_000   // 7% of last price
```

If the reported price deviates from the last stored price by more than 7%:

- **Price returns are skipped** — no PnL is transferred between pools for this update.
- **Funding still settles** — the elapsed time is used to accrue and pay out funding normally.
- **The timestamp advances** — `lastTimestamp` is updated so future funding accrual is correct.
- **The price is NOT updated** — `lastPrice` retains its previous value, meaning the full move will only take effect once it has been reported within the 7% band.

<Warning>
  If you observe a very large price move that you expect to produce PnL, check whether the circuit breaker has triggered. Your position's price-return P&L may be deferred until subsequent oracle updates clear the move within permitted increments.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  The circuit breaker is deliberately asymmetric in its impact: it suppresses returns but not funding. Payers continue to accrue funding income even during a circuit-breaker event.
</Tip>

## No Liquidations — Losses Bounded by Pool Size

Unlike margin-based derivatives, Null V1 has no liquidation engine. You cannot lose more than the capital you have deposited into a pool. The net settlement formula enforces this hard cap:

```
U = returnsPayerToReceiver - fundingReceiverToPayer   // signed net
N = max(-receiverPool, min(U, payerPool))
```

Here:

- **U** is the uncapped net transfer (positive = payer owes receiver; negative = receiver owes payer).
- **N** is the capped transfer actually applied.
  - `min(U, payerPool)` ensures the payer cannot pay out more than it holds.
  - `max(-receiverPool, ...)` ensures the receiver cannot pay out more than it holds.

<Info>
  Funding and returns are **netted before capping**. This means a receiver who is simultaneously earning price returns and paying funding only moves the difference — preserving the whitepaper model and avoiding double-counting edge cases.
</Info>

A worked example:

| Scenario | Returns (U\_returns) | Funding (U\_funding) | Net U | Capped N |
|----------|---------------------|---------------------|-------|----------|
| Strong price rally, low funding | +$1,000 | −$50 | +$950 | min(950, payerPool) |
| Price flat, high funding owed | $0 | −$200 | −$200 | max(−200, −receiverPool) |
| Price drop larger than funding | −$500 | −$100 | −$600 | max(−600, −receiverPool) |

## State Update Order

Every deposit, redeem, or oracle interaction triggers a full state update. Understanding the order matters for reasoning about when your PnL settles.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Validate oracle price">
    The new price from the oracle must be non-zero. If it is zero, the entire transaction reverts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply yield fee">
    If the market uses yield-bearing collateral, the protocol's share of accrued yield is deducted before computing returns. See [Yield-Bearing Collateral](/mechanics/yield-bearing-collateral).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconcile external balance changes">
    Any tokens that arrived in the contract outside of normal deposits (e.g. direct transfers or donations) are folded into the pool balances.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply netted funding + returns">
    The net transfer **N** is computed and applied to both pool balances simultaneously.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store new timestamp and price">
    `lastTimestamp` and `lastPrice` are updated. The circuit breaker check uses these values on the next update.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Practical Implications for Integrators

- **Every deposit and redeem settles PnL.** You cannot deposit or withdraw without first settling the accrued funding and price returns since the last checkpoint.
- **Large positions amplify circuit-breaker deferral.** If you hold a large receiver position and the circuit breaker fires repeatedly, accrued price returns may be deferred across many oracle updates.
- **Pool size is your maximum loss.** Design your collateral sizing based on the worst-case price move you are willing to absorb — there is no margin call to force additional deposits.
- **Net settlement favors efficiency.** Because returns and funding net before capping, you avoid scenarios where a receiver pool is drained by funding even while it is simultaneously owed price-return profits.

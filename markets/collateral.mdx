Null V1 markets use one ERC-20 token as collateral. The factory accepts the market asset address at creation, and all core deposits, redemptions, pool balances, fees, and settlement transfers are denominated in that token.

---

## ERC-20 Collateral

Any standard ERC-20 token can serve as collateral for a Null V1 market. There is no whitelist in the factory beyond the non-zero address check. Integrators should still choose assets carefully: decimals, transfer behavior, rebasing behavior, and external risk all affect user experience.

## Balance Reconciliation

Before every state-changing pool action, the market compares its actual token balance with the recorded Receiver and Payer pool balances. If the actual balance is higher, the external gain is allocated pro rata across both pools. If the actual balance is lower, the external loss is deducted pro rata.

This is how Null accounts for donations, rebases, fee-on-transfer effects, or other balance changes that happen outside explicit pool deposits and redemptions.

<Tip>
  If the collateral token's `balanceOf(market)` grows over time, that growth is
  reflected through reconciliation. The market does not track a collateral
  share-price checkpoint.
</Tip>

---

## Token Selection Risks

Reconciliation keeps accounting tied to actual collateral, but it does not make every ERC-20 equally good collateral. Rebasing tokens, fee-on-transfer tokens, tokens with blacklists, and tokens with external strategy risk can change market outcomes in ways users need to understand.

---

## Depositing and Withdrawing

The core pools accept the market asset directly. If the market asset is an ERC-4626 vault share, direct pool callers deposit and withdraw vault shares.

`NullV1Router` can make ERC-4626 markets easier for users: on deposit it accepts the vault underlying and wraps it into the market asset; on redemption it unwraps the market asset and returns the underlying.

<Note>
  Receiver and Payer positions are still ERC-4626-compatible pool shares. This
  ERC-4626 behavior belongs to the Null pool tokens, not to the market
  collateral asset.
</Note>

## Choosing Collateral: Summary

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Use an asset when..." icon="circle-check">
    - Users understand the collateral risk
    - You want the simplest possible integration
    - The token has predictable transfer behavior
    - The oracle and collateral denomination make sense together
  </Card>
  <Card title="Be careful when..." icon="triangle-alert">
    - Balances can rebase or change externally
    - Transfers charge fees or have hooks
    - The collateral relies on an external yield strategy
    - The token has admin controls that can affect transfers
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

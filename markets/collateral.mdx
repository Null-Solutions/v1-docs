Null V1 markets use one ERC-20 token as collateral. The market, its receiver
pool, and its payer pool all account in units of that token.

---

## ERC-20 Collateral

The factory only checks that the collateral address is nonzero. Its interface
documents stronger assumptions: transfers should be non-reentrant, and sender
and receiver balances should change by exactly the requested amount. A token
passing the factory check is not therefore guaranteed to be safe collateral.

## Balance Reconciliation

During state settlement, the market reconciles
`collateral.balanceOf(address(market))` against its internal accounting before
applying collateral fees, funding, and price PnL. This runs on pool
deposit/mint/withdraw/redeem and on fee sweeps; there is no standalone public
poke.

Protocol fees are reserved first. If the recorded protocol fees exceed the
actual balance, they are capped to that balance and no collateral remains for
the pools.

The remaining balance is compared with `receiverPool + payerPool`:

- a surplus is allocated between pools in proportion to their recorded assets
- a deficit is deducted proportionally, with integer-rounding remainder charged
  to the larger pool first
- if both pool balances are zero, any remaining collateral is recorded as
  protocol fees

This captures donations and rebases in pool accounting. It does not make
fee-on-transfer or otherwise non-standard tokens compliant with the factory's
documented transfer assumptions.

<Tip>
  Reconciliation uses collateral-token units. It does not separately track the
  value of the collateral in an underlying asset.
</Tip>

## Token Selection Risks

Rebasing tokens, fee-on-transfer tokens, blacklists, hooks, and external
strategy risk can change outcomes or break expected transfers. Review token
behavior rather than relying on the factory's nonzero-address check.

---

## Depositing and Withdrawing

The core pools accept the market collateral directly. If the collateral is an
ERC-4626 vault, direct pool callers deposit and withdraw vault shares.

Both `NullV1Router` and `NullV1PayableRouter` optimistically detect ERC-4626
collateral by calling `asset()`. On deposit, the router:

1. pulls the requested amount of underlying from the user
2. deposits it into the collateral vault
3. deposits the resulting vault shares into the selected Null pool

On redemption, it receives collateral vault shares from the Null pool, redeems
them through the vault, and sends the underlying to the user. Router asset
amounts and slippage checks are therefore in underlying units for ERC-4626
collateral, but in collateral-token units for plain ERC-20 collateral.

If `asset()` reverts, the router treats the token as a plain ERC-20. A token
that exposes `asset()` but is not a conforming ERC-4626 vault may fail later in
the flow.

<Note>
  Receiver and payer positions are themselves ERC-4626-compatible Null pool
  shares. They are distinct from an ERC-4626 vault used as market collateral.
</Note>

## Choosing Collateral: Summary

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Use an asset when..." icon="circle-check">
    - Users understand the collateral risk
    - You want the simplest possible integration
    - The token has predictable transfer behavior
    - The oracle and collateral denomination make sense together
  </Card>
  <Card title="Be careful when..." icon="triangle-alert">
    - Balances can rebase or change externally
    - Transfers charge fees or have hooks
    - The collateral relies on an external yield strategy
    - The token has admin controls that can affect transfers
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

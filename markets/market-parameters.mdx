A Null V1 market is governed by a small set of parameters, some of which are fixed permanently at creation and others that can be updated by the protocol owner over the market's lifetime. Understanding each parameter, especially `mu`, the funding multiplier, is essential before you deploy or integrate with a market. This page describes every field exposed by `NullV1Market`, explains the economics behind each one, and provides worked examples to help you choose sensible values.

---

## Immutable Parameters

These three parameters are set at market creation and can never be changed. They uniquely identify the market and define its fundamental economics.

<ParamField path="asset" type="address" required>
  The ERC-20 token used as collateral in this market. All deposits, withdrawals,
  and pool balances are denominated in this token. See [Collateral](/markets/collateral)
  for integration notes.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="oracle" type="address" required>
  The `INullV1Oracle` implementation that prices the market's underlying asset.
  All oracles return prices scaled to `1e36`. The oracle address becomes part of
  the market's identity tuple `(asset, oracle, mu)`. See
  [Oracles](/markets/oracles) for available implementations.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="mu" type="uint24" required>
  The funding multiplier, expressed in **parts-per-million (ppm)**. This is the
  single most important economic parameter in the market. It controls how
  aggressively funding rates scale with pool imbalance. `mu` is immutable; if
  you need different economics, deploy a new market.
</ParamField>

---

## Pool Addresses

<ParamField path="receiverPool" type="address">
  The address of the receiver-side liquidity pool. Liquidity providers in this
  pool receive the reference asset return and pay funding to the Payer pool.
  These addresses are deployed automatically by the factory at market creation
  time and are emitted in the `MarketCreated` event.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="payerPool" type="address">
  The address of the payer-side liquidity pool. Liquidity providers in this pool
  back Receiver exposure, receive funding, and take the opposite price risk.
</ParamField>

---

## Fee Parameters

Fee values are set by the protocol owner and can be updated post-deployment, subject to protocol-enforced maximums.

<ParamField path="entryFee" type="uint16">
  Fee charged when liquidity enters a pool, expressed in **basis points** (1 bp =
  0.01%). Maximum value: **500 bp (5%)**. Entry fees are routed to the
  protocol owner and leave the Receiver/Payer pool system.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="exitFee" type="uint16">
  Fee charged when liquidity exits a pool, expressed in **basis points**.
  Maximum value: **500 bp (5%)**. Exit fees are routed to the protocol owner
  and are not redistributed between the two pools.
</ParamField>

## Understanding mu

`mu` is the parameter you will spend the most time thinking about when creating a market. It is the lever that sets the relationship between pool imbalance (`E`) and funding rates.

### The Funding Formula

The annual funding rate paid by the receiver pool is:

```
fundingApr = min(mu / 1_000_000 * E, 100%)
```

Where:
- **`mu`** is the funding multiplier in ppm
- **`E`** is the ratio of receiver pool TVL to payer pool TVL (`receiverTVL / payerTVL`)
- The receiver pool **pays** `fundingApr` per year to the payer pool
- The payer pool **earns** `fundingApr * E` per year (because it is smaller by definition when `E > 1`)

### Choosing mu

Use this formula to derive the right `mu` for your target economics:

```
mu = (targetReceiverFundingApr / targetE) * 1_000_000
```

For example, if you want Receivers to pay 3% APR when `E = 3`:

```
mu = (0.03 / 3) * 1_000_000 = 10_000
```

<Tip>
  Think of `mu` as the funding rate per unit of pool imbalance. A higher `mu`
  makes the market more reactive to imbalance, which can suit volatile or
  high-conviction assets. A lower `mu` creates a more stable funding environment
  suited to broad indexes.
</Tip>

### Common mu Values

| mu value | ppm % | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|
| `3_000` | 0.3% | Broad index RWAs (e.g. S&P 500 tracker) |
| `10_000` | 1.0% | Single-stock RWAs (e.g. AAPL, TSLA) |
| `20_000` | 2.0% | High-yield or volatile RWAs |

## mu Example Tables

The tables below show how funding rates vary with `E` for two common `mu` settings. "Buffer" is the percentage of total TVL in the Payer pool; a smaller buffer means higher price sensitivity for Payers.

### mu = 10,000 (1%) - Single-Stock RWA (e.g. AAPL)

| E | Buffer | Receiver pays | Payer earns |
|-----|--------|---------------|-------------|
| 2   | 50%    | 2%            | 4%          |
| 3   | 33%    | 3%            | 9%          |
| 4   | 25%    | 4%            | 16%         |
| 5   | 20%    | 5%            | 25%         |

### mu = 3,000 (0.3%) - Broad Index RWA (e.g. S&P 500)

| E     | Buffer | Receiver pays | Payer earns |
|-------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| 4     | 25%    | 1.2%          | 4.8%        |
| 5     | 20%    | 1.5%          | 7.5%        |
| 6.67  | 15%    | 2.0%          | 13.3%       |

<Note>
  Payer earnings scale quadratically with `E` because Payers receive funding
  proportional to both the receiver pool size (which determines the total
  funding paid) and the inverse of their own pool size (which determines their
  per-unit share). This asymmetric payoff is intentional: payers take on price
  risk and are compensated accordingly.
</Note>

## mu Is Immutable

Once a market is created, `mu` cannot be changed. This immutability is by design: it makes market behaviour predictable and prevents governance from altering the economics of existing positions.

<Warning>
  If you deploy a market with an incorrect `mu` value, the only remedy is to
  deploy a new market with a different `(asset, oracle, mu)` tuple. The original
  market will continue to exist and operate indefinitely with its original
  parameters.
</Warning>

If you are unsure which `mu` to use, start with a conservative value, then model the funding rates at your expected `E` using the formula above before committing to a deployment.

## Reading Parameters On-Chain

You can inspect all parameters on a live market by calling these view functions directly on the `NullV1Market` contract:

```solidity
INullV1Market market = INullV1Market(marketAddress);

address asset       = address(market.asset());        // collateral token
address oracle      = address(market.oracle());       // price oracle
uint24  mu          = market.mu();                    // funding multiplier (ppm)
address receiverPool = address(market.receiverPool()); // receiver LP pool
address payerPool   = address(market.payerPool());    // payer LP pool
uint16  entryFee    = market.entryFee();              // basis points
uint16  exitFee     = market.exitFee();               // basis points
```

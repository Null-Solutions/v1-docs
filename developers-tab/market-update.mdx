Every state-changing pool action calls `market.updateState()` before it mints shares, burns shares, or records user balance changes.

The market delegates settlement math to `NullV1Math.computeUpdate`.

## Execution order

The implementation order is:

1. Validate the oracle price.
2. Reconcile the actual market collateral balance against recorded accounting.
3. Apply the annualized collateral fee.
4. Compute funding and price settlement as a single net transfer.
5. Store the new timestamp and oracle price.

In code terms:

```solidity
updated = _reconcileExternalBalanceChanges(state, balance);
updated = _updatePoolsForCollateralFee(updated, collateralFee);
updated = _updatePoolsForFundingAndReturns(updated, price, mu);
updated.timestamp = block.timestamp;
updated.price = price;
```

## Price validation

The oracle price must be nonzero. Null oracles normalize prices to `1e36`.

If the oracle price is zero, the update reverts. The market is not partially updated.

## Reconciliation

The market passes `asset.balanceOf(address(this))` into the math library. The library excludes `protocolFees` and compares remaining actual collateral against recorded Receiver plus Payer pool balances.

If actual collateral is higher than recorded pool value, the gain is allocated proportionally across Receiver and Payer.

If actual collateral is lower than recorded pool value, the loss is deducted proportionally. Any rounding remainder is deducted from the largest pool first.

If both pools are empty, actual collateral is treated as protocol fees. This avoids dividing by zero and prevents unassigned assets from being redistributed into empty pools later.

## Collateral fee

After reconciliation, the market applies `collateralFee` to both pool balances based on elapsed time:

```text
pool fee = pool balance * collateralFee / 10,000 * elapsed / 365 days
```

The fee is annualized, capped by the available pool balance, and accrued into `protocolFees`. It is charged on collateral principal over time rather than as a percentage of measured yield.

Entry and exit fees follow the same model: they are deducted from deposits and redemptions and added to `protocolFees` inside the market rather than being transferred to the protocol owner on each call. The protocol owner pulls all accrued fees — collateral, entry, and exit — out of the market in a single transfer with `sweepFees()`. Keeping fees inside the market on the hot path means a deposit or redemption cannot be blocked by a token that restricts transfers to the protocol owner (for example a USDC blacklist).

## Funding

Funding is paid from Receiver to Payer.

The funding APR is:

```text
fundingApr = min(receiverPool / payerPool * mu / 1e6, 100%)
```

The funding amount is scaled by elapsed time:

```text
funding = receiverPool * fundingApr * elapsed / 365 days
```

Funding returns zero if:

- `block.timestamp <= previous timestamp`;
- either pool is empty;
- the computed funding APR is zero.

## Price settlement

Price settlement compares the new oracle price to the previous stored price.

The raw price transfer is based on Receiver notional:

```text
receiverNotional = receiverPool * 1e36 / previousPrice
receiverPnl = priceDelta * receiverNotional / 1e36
```

If the price is up, the transfer is from Payer to Receiver. If the price is down, the transfer is from Receiver to Payer.

## Circuit breaker

The implementation computes a time-scaled maximum price delta:

```text
maxAllowedDelta = previousPrice * 700 * elapsed / (10_000 * 1 hour)
```

If the actual absolute price delta exceeds `maxAllowedDelta`, the price-transfer leg returns zero for that update.

Funding still applies, and the market stores the new price at the end of the update.

## Netted solvency bound

Funding and price settlement are netted before collateral moves between pools.

```text
net transfer into Receiver = price transfer from Payer to Receiver - funding from Receiver to Payer
```

If the net transfer is positive, Receiver receives value and Payer loses value. The transfer is capped at `payerPool`.

If the net transfer is negative, Receiver pays value and Payer receives value. The transfer is capped at `receiverPool`.

This matches the whitepaper invariant:

```text
N = max(-R, min(U, P))
```

where `U` is the unconstrained net transfer into Receiver, `R` is the reconciled Receiver pool, and `P` is the reconciled Payer pool.

## Persistence and events

After settlement, the market stores the updated `MarketState` and emits `StateUpdated`:

```solidity
event StateUpdated(
    uint256 indexed timestamp,
    uint256 receiverPoolAssets,
    uint256 payerPoolAssets,
    uint256 protocolFees,
    uint256 price
);
```

Pool actions continue only after this state has been persisted.

## Integration implications

- Previews can become stale because execution updates the market first.
- Indexers should treat `StateUpdated` as the canonical accounting checkpoint.
- Empty or one-sided markets do not settle normal funding or price transfers.
- The circuit breaker suppresses directional price settlement, not funding.
- Solvency is applied to the netted transfer, not to funding and price movement separately.

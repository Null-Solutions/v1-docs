The circuit breaker limits how far a single settlement can move value between the pools. If the oracle price has moved more than 20% since the last settlement, the price transfer for that settlement is skipped and the new price becomes the baseline.

## The rule

During settlement, the market compares the fresh oracle price with the price stored at the previous settlement.

| Move since last settlement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 20% or less | The price transfer settles in full. |
| More than 20% | The price transfer is skipped in full. The new price is stored as the baseline. |

There is no partial transfer up to the boundary. A move settles completely or not at all.

| Constant | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| `MAX_ABS_CHANGE_BPS` | 2000 (20%) |

The threshold is the same for every market and does not depend on how much time has passed since the last settlement.

## Why it exists

The breaker protects both pools from two things an oracle can produce that are not real price moves:

- **Corporate actions.** A 2-for-1 split halves the reported price overnight. Settled mechanically, that would hand half the Receiver pool to Payers for a move that never happened economically. A 4-for-1 split would take three quarters.
- **Faulty prints.** A bad report that lands onchain can be back-run by searchers in the same block, draining one side before anyone can react.

Traditional equity swaps handle the same events through the adjustment and market disruption provisions in ISDA's equity definitions. The contract cannot tell a split from a glitch or a crash, so it treats every jump above 20% in one settlement the same way: as a new starting point, not as profit and loss.

The rule applies to both sides equally. On a skipped rise, Receivers do not receive the gain and Payers do not pay it. On a skipped fall, Receivers do not take the loss and Payers do not receive it.

## When it fires

- The price transfer for that settlement is skipped.
- The fresh price and timestamp are stored. The next settlement measures from the new price.
- Funding still settles, subject to the pool floor in [Settlement](/protocol/settlement).
- The deposit or redemption that triggered settlement proceeds at the existing pool values.
- `UpdateState` is emitted with `circuitBreakerFired` set to `true`.

The skipped move is not carried forward, averaged into later settlements, or paid out later. No participant can claim it.

## Examples

A market's stored price is 100.

| Path | Settlements | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 → 125 → 130 | 100 → 125 is 25%: skipped. 125 → 130 is 4%: settled. | Only the move from 125 to 130 is transferred between the pools. |
| 100 → 112 → 125 | 100 → 112 is 12%: settled. 112 → 125 is 11.6%: settled. | The full move from 100 to 125 is transferred. |

The same price path can settle in full or be partly skipped, depending on how often the market settles while it unfolds.

## Limits

- **Per settlement, not cumulative.** Consecutive moves below 20% each settle in full. Two settlements of 19% move the price about 42% between them. The breaker is not a bound on losses.
- **Large moves only.** A wrong price within 20% of the stored price settles in full.
- **No price, no settlement.** A zero price or a reverting oracle stops settlement before the breaker is checked. See [Oracles](/contracts/oracles/overview).

## Dormant markets

Settlement runs only when someone deposits or redeems. If the price moves more than 20% while nobody settles, the side that would lose can send a dust deposit to trigger settlement and skip the move. Either side can do this.

Frequent settlement prevents it. Any deposit or redemption settles the market, so regular activity, keepers and interfaces keep the gap between settlements well under 20%. Only genuine single-print jumps then trip the breaker.

## For holders and integrators

- Pool shares are valued from settled state. After a trip, a share is still a claim on the same collateral, even if an external chart shows the asset has moved.
- To change exposure after a trip, resize, exit or re-enter at settled values, paying the entry and exit fees in [Fees](/protocol/fees).
- Indexers and interfaces read `circuitBreakerFired` on `UpdateState` to show that a price move was skipped. See [Indexer](/contracts/integration/indexer).

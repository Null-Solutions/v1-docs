## You hold your own assets

Null is self-directed and non-custodial, and both halves of that phrase carry weight. You connect a wallet you control, you decide whether
to submit each transaction, and you sign it yourself. Null does not hold your private keys or recovery credentials, does not custody or
control the assets in your wallet, and exercises no discretion over your orders or positions.

The consequences follow from that design rather than from policy. Null cannot guarantee that a transaction will execute or settle as the
preview suggested, and it cannot recover assets, reverse a confirmed transaction, or compensate a loss. Smart contracts do what they do
once a transaction confirms onchain, and that is often irreversible. Check before you sign, because after you sign there may be no way
back.

## Fees, gas, and costs elsewhere

Entry and exit fees, where set, are charged on notional. Fees paid on the Receiver side credit the Payer pool rather than Null. Fees paid
on the Payer side are protocol fees and reach the factory owner. There is no fee for holding a position. Funding moving between Receiver and Payer
is part of protocol settlement, not a fee paid to Null.

Everything else still costs money. You are responsible for Ethereum gas and for any wallet, transfer, onramp, off ramp, collateral,
oracle, venue, hedging, tax, or third party costs your activity creates. A hedge held at an external venue, in particular, bills you
entirely outside Null's accounting.

## The risks that matter most

Position risk comes first. Receiver and Payer values can fall substantially, and a Payer position carries short scale, so thinner coverage
means larger percentage exposure to every price move. Funding is paid from variable collateral yield, which means the rate that attracted
you can shrink. Leveraged positions add Morpho and Margin risk, and a position that reaches its book's LLTV can be liquidated.

The machinery carries its own risks. Smart contracts can contain bugs or behave in ways nobody intended. An oracle can report an incorrect
or stale price, stop reporting, or diverge from the market you care about, and settlement can be affected or blocked as a result. The
collateral and the Morpho vault behind it can lose value or become hard to redeem.

Then there is the risk of simply being unable to act. Entry, exit, transfers, Savings withdrawals, lending recalls, and hedging liquidity
can all be unavailable when you want them. Settlement, slippage, deadlines, and gas can change an outcome between preview and
confirmation, and transactions can fail outright. A hedge adds its own funding, basis, margin, liquidation, custody, and operational
risks. Finally, regulatory and tax treatment varies by person and jurisdiction and can change after you have acted.
[Risks](/how-null-works/what-can-go-wrong) develops each of these in more depth.

## What an audit does and does not do

Nethermind Security reviewed a defined scope of the Null V1 contracts, and that scope is the whole story. A completed audit is evidence
that specified code was examined, not a guarantee that the code is correct or that outcomes are safe. Immutability and onchain visibility
do not change that. Later contracts, including Margin routing, can sit entirely outside the report, so read [Audits](/protocol/audits) and
match its scope against the product you are actually using.

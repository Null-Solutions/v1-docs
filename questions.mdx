<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is Null audited?">
    Yes. Nethermind audited the core contracts in August 2026, and the report is public on Nethermind's GitHub. Some newer contracts came after that review. [Audits](/protocol/audits) lists exactly what it covered.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why not a perp?">
    A perp charges funding on your whole position. On Null you only give up your deposit's interest. There is no liquidation price on Spot.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What does it cost?">
    Each market has its own entry and exit fees, capped at 2.5%. Receivers and Payers can have different fees. There is no fee for holding. Gas is separate. See [Fees](/how-null-works/what-it-costs).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who holds my money?">
    You do. Your tokens stay in your own wallet, and the collateral stays in the contracts. Null never holds your funds.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where do prices come from?">
    Chainlink price feeds. Any single jump above 20% is skipped, not settled. See [Circuit breaker](/protocol/circuit-breaker).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why are jumps above 20% skipped?">
    To protect both sides from moves that are not real, such as a stock split halving the price or a faulty oracle print that searchers could drain the pools on. Any single move above 20% becomes the new starting point instead of profit and loss, for Receivers and Payers alike. See [Why it exists](/protocol/circuit-breaker#why-it-exists).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I own the stock or coin a market follows?">
    No. You hold a Receiver Pool share, a vault share or a Morpho position, depending on the product. No votes or dividends reach you, unless the price feed itself includes them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do Payers always have enough to pay Receivers?">
    Every gain is paid from collateral already in the Payer pool. Nothing can owe more than it holds. A **Buy** or a **Payer sell** cannot leave the Receiver pool more than five times the Payer pool, so whenever someone joins as a Receiver or leaves as a Payer, the Payer pool covers at least a 20% rise. A rally can still push past that. New Receivers and leaving Payers then wait until the ratio comes back down. See [Pool ratio cap](/protocol/pool-ratio-cap).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there an incentive for Payers to join when they're needed?">
    Yes, and it's built in. The Payer pool works like the margin behind a short the size of the whole Receiver pool. Payers earn funding on that full notional, plus interest on their own margin. The thinner the Payer pool, the more each Payer dollar earns: with 5% interest, Payers earn about 10% on their capital when the pools are equal, and about 30% at the cap of five. Payers who hedge elsewhere want to post as little margin as possible, so they're drawn in exactly when Payers are scarce. See [Why scarce Payers earn more](/payers#why-scarce-payers-earn-more).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the Payer pool runs out?">
    Returns stop until new Payers join. Missed price moves are not made up later. The [pool ratio cap](/protocol/pool-ratio-cap) makes this less likely. It does not rule it out.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why can't I sell my Payer position?">
    The Receiver pool can be at most five times the Payer pool, and a **Payer sell** cannot take it past that. You can sell the part above that line now. The rest waits until Receivers sell or new Payers join. See [Leaving can wait](/payers#leaving-can-wait).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I get out?">
    Sell your pool tokens for your share of the collateral, less fees. Savings and Lending return what borrowers aren't using right away, and the rest as loans are repaid.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if Null shuts down?">
    The contracts keep running without Null's app.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How can my synthetic earn interest?">
    Lend it into its short market through [Lending](/products/lending). Leverage Short traders borrow it, and you keep its price exposure while it's lent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between Savings and Lending?">
    Savings is managed: **Add money** puts USDC into the Null Savings Vault, which lends USDCn across every long market for you. Lending is direct: you pick a market and use **Lend** and **Recall**, including lending your synthetics to short markets.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between Spot and Margin?">
    Spot buys a pool share outright. It can lose value but can never be liquidated. Margin borrows on Morpho to make the position bigger, which brings a liquidation price at the 96.5% LLTV.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where is Payer?">
    Turn on **Pro Mode** in the account menu. **Payer buy** and **Payer sell** then appear on the Spot ticket.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does funding always flow to Payers?">
    Funding is the interest earned on Receiver deposits, passed to Payers for backing them. Its source never changes, so its direction never flips. See [How Payers earn](/how-null-works/payers-earn-interest-and-fees).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which markets are live?">
    The list on `/trade` is the live set. These docs use AAPLr, SPXr, BTCr and GOLDr as examples only.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What are the risks?">
    Bad price feeds, contract bugs, collateral losses, and liquidation if you borrow. Audits reduce risk. They do not remove it. See [Risks](/how-null-works/what-can-go-wrong).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

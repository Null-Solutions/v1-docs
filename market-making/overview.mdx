Market making on Null means holding **Payer**. You provide the collateral that backs Receiver. If the oracle price rises, Payer loses. If it falls, Payer gains. You also receive funding paid from Receiver-side collateral yield.

Turn on **Pro Mode** to see **Payer buy** and **Payer sell**.

## What you actually hold

A Payer position is a negative-size position in the Receiver pool, with the market's collateral inside that structure. Your short notional is your position value times `R / P`. You do not pick that multiple. The pools set it, and it moves.

```text
short scale = R / P
Payer short notional = p × R / P
```

Size the external hedge off that notional, and resize it when the pools move. A hedge entered at one scale is the wrong size after coverage changes.

## What you earn

Approximate Payer carry, before fees and hedge costs:

```text
y × (1 + R / P)
```

`y` is collateral yield. The `y` term is yield on your own collateral. The `y × R / P` term is funding received from Receivers. Worked numbers: [Funding and yield](/concepts/funding-and-yield).

## The hedge is not Null funding

Many Payers buy the tracked asset, or a perpetual, somewhere else so price risk offsets. What remains is Null funding, plus yield on Payer collateral, minus the cost of that hedge.

External venues charge their own funding, fees, and maintenance margin. Those charges are not Funding APR. A positive Funding APR can still lose money after hedge funding and basis.

Null does not open or manage that hedge for you.

## Operating checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the pools">
    Check `R`, `P`, collateral APR, and funding APR on the market before you buy Payer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buy Payer">
    Use **Payer buy** in Pro Mode. The fee is on notional, so it scales with `R / P`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hedge outside, if you choose">
    Match short notional `p × R / P`. Recheck after large pool flows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch carry">
    Compare Null funding and collateral yield with external funding and fees. Exit with **Payer sell** when the trade no longer pays.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Payer can lose most of its value when the oracle rises and coverage is thin. Funding does not cap that loss.
</Warning>

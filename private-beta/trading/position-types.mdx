Every Null market has two pools:

- **Receiver** follows the tracked asset's price return.
- **Payer** provides the backing for Receiver and takes the opposite side of the price movement.

When you mint, you deposit collateral into your selected pool and receive pool shares. When you burn, you redeem those shares for the collateral available from that pool.

## Compare the two sides

| Question | Receiver | Payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What price exposure do I take? | Position value increases when the tracked price rises and decreases when it falls, subject to settlement limits. | Takes the opposite side: position value decreases when the tracked price rises and increases when it falls, subject to settlement limits. |
| How large can the price effect be? | Receiver's price return generally follows the tracked price move before funding, fees, and other effects. | Payer leverage is `L = R / P`. Lower coverage means higher leverage, so the same price move has a larger percentage impact on Payer share value. |
| How does funding work? | Pays when the signed funding rate is positive. Receives when it is negative. | Receives funding when the rate is positive and pays when it is negative. Each holder receives or pays a proportional share based on their Payer position. |
| Do I own the tracked asset? | No. You own ERC-4626 Receiver pool shares. | No. You own ERC-4626 Payer pool shares. |
| What do I receive when I exit? | Your share of the collateral available in the Receiver pool, after the exit fee. | Your share of the collateral available in the Payer pool, after the exit fee. |
| What should I check first? | Oracle, price direction, funding, collateral, fees, and both pool balances. | Payer coverage, funding, collateral, fees, and how a price move could affect the Payer pool. |

## Worked price example

Suppose a market has 1,000 collateral units in Receiver and 200 in Payer. The tracked price then rises by 2%.

```text
Price transfer = 2% × 1,000 = 20 units

Receiver: 1,000 → 1,020  (+2%)
Payer:      200 →   180  (-10%)
```

The same 20 units move between the pools. The percentage impact is larger on Payer because its pool is smaller. If the tracked price fell by 2%, the transfer would normally run in the other direction.

This example excludes funding, fees, collateral yield, reconciliation, and settlement bounds. It shows how price exposure works, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Add funding to the decision

Funding accrues between market updates and is combined with price movement when the market settles.

- A **positive** funding rate transfers value from Receiver to Payer.
- A **negative** funding rate transfers value from Payer to Receiver.
- If either pool is empty, the standard model applies neither funding nor price movement.

Funding can reduce or increase the impact of price movement. It is variable, not fixed yield. The protocol applies the funding rate to Receiver notional, then distributes the transfer proportionally across Payer shares.

Your final result can also be affected by collateral performance, entry and exit fees, liquidity, oracle behavior, and pool state.

<Warning>
Receiver and Payer are pool positions, not conventional order-book orders. The app does not offer a user-selected leverage setting.
</Warning>

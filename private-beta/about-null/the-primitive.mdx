Null is a **two-pool system**. Every market has exactly two pools that face each other:

- **Receiver** takes the oracle-tracked price return.
- **Payer** backs that return and takes the other side.

Both pools deposit the same collateral token into one market. The market holds the collateral and moves value between the pools when it settles. The protocol does not lend the tracked asset, open margin accounts, or liquidate individual users.

```text
                    ┌─────────────────┐
   collateral ──▶   │     Market      │   ◀── collateral
                    │  (holds funds)  │
                    └────────┬────────┘
               ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
               ▼                           ▼
        Receiver pool                 Payer pool
        (ERC-4626 shares)             (ERC-4626 shares)
```

## How the two pools work together

Let `R` be Receiver pool assets and `P` be Payer pool assets.

- When the accepted oracle price **rises**, value normally moves from Payer → Receiver.
- When it **falls**, value normally moves from Receiver → Payer.
- **Funding** also moves between the pools based on how much Payer coverage exists (`P / R`).

Settlement can only redistribute collateral the market already holds, subject to a minimum-pool floor. Nobody borrows the tracked asset.

<Info>
You do not own or receive the tracked asset. A Receiver position does not include voting rights, dividends, or other ownership benefits unless the market's oracle return explicitly reflects them.
</Info>

## Positions

A position is your share of one pool. You pick a side, deposit collateral, and receive transferable ERC-4626 shares in that pool. Your shares are a percentage of the whole pool — not a bilateral trade with a fixed entry price.

### Receiver

Receiver takes the tracked price return against its pool assets. A 5% accepted price increase credits Receiver by about 5% of Receiver assets. A 5% decrease debits it by about 5%, before funding, fees, collateral changes, and settlement bounds.

### Payer

Payer supplies the reserve that backs Receiver. It takes the opposite price transfer and earns or pays funding according to coverage.

Payer's **instantaneous leverage** is:

```text
L = R / P
```

`L` is not chosen by the trader and is not fixed at entry. It changes whenever either pool's assets change. A Payer holder with current position value `p` has notional:

```text
Payer notional = p * L = p * R / P
```

If coverage falls, leverage rises and the same Receiver-side transfer has a larger percentage effect on Payer share value.

## Worked examples

These examples ignore funding, fees, collateral changes, rounding, and settlement bounds unless noted. Start from `R = 1,000` Receiver assets.

### Price rises

Suppose `P = 200` and the accepted oracle price rises by 2%. The price transfer is about 2% of Receiver assets, or 20 collateral units:

```text
Receiver: 1,000 → 1,020
Payer:      200 →   180
```

Payer leverage is `L = 1,000 / 200 = 5×`. Receiver gains 2%. Payer pays the same 20-unit transfer, which is a 10% loss on its 200-unit pool:

```text
Payer return ≈ -price move * L ≈ -2% * 5 ≈ -10%
```

### Price falls

Same pools, accepted oracle price falls by 2%. The transfer reverses:

```text
Receiver: 1,000 →   980
Payer:      200 →   220
```

Receiver loses 2%. Payer gains the same 20 units — about +10% at `5×` leverage.

### Same move, different coverage

A 2% accepted price rise always moves about `0.02 * R` from Payer to Receiver. Coverage changes how hard that hits Payer:

| Payer `P` | Coverage `P/R` | Leverage `L` | Transfer | Receiver | Payer return |
| --------: | -------------: | -----------: | -------: | -------: | -----------: |
|       500 |            50% |          2× |       20 |     +2% |         -4% |
|       200 |            20% |          5× |       20 |     +2% |        -10% |
|       100 |            10% |         10× |       20 |     +2% |        -20% |

Lower coverage means higher Payer leverage and a larger percentage impact on Payer for the same oracle move.

### Your share of a pool

Pools are shared. If the Payer pool is `P = 200` and you hold `50` of it (25% of the pool), you take 25% of every Payer-side transfer.

After the +2% price example above (`P: 200 → 180`):

```text
Your Payer claim: 50 → 45
Your return:      -10%  (same as the pool)
```

Minting or burning by others changes pool size and your percentage. Your share of the next transfer changes with it.

### Funding at different coverage

Using the standard model (`c = 0.5`, `mu = 1`) and `R = 1,000`. Values are annualized before time prorating:

| Payer `P` | Coverage | Funding APR `f` | Annual transfer | Direction |
| --------: | -------: | --------------: | --------------: | --------- |
|       100 |      10% |          +66.7% |             667 | Receiver → Payer |
|       500 |      50% |              0% |               0 | None |
|     1,000 |     100% |          -33.3% |             333 | Payer → Receiver |

At `P = 100`, a Payer holder with `p = 25` (25% of the pool) has notional `p * L = 25 * 10 = 250`, so their annualized funding receipt is about `250 * 66.7% ≈ 167` before settlement caps and elapsed-time prorating.

Funding can reverse as coverage moves through the 50% target. It is not guaranteed yield.

## Funding and coverage

The funding-rate model encourages the market toward a target amount of Payer coverage:

- below target coverage, funding can flow from Receiver to Payer;
- above target coverage, funding can flow from Payer to Receiver; and
- at target coverage, the standard model's funding rate is zero.

Define Payer coverage as `q = P / R`. The standard model calculates:

```text
f = mu * (c - q) / (c + q)
```

`c` is the target coverage and `mu` controls the curve's maximum magnitude. The standard deployment uses `c = 0.5` and `mu = 1`:

Funding is positive below 50% coverage, zero at 50%, and negative above 50%. The curve is annualized and bounded by `-mu` and `+mu`. If either pool is empty, funding and price PnL are both zero.

If `f` is the signed market funding APR, it applies to Receiver notional:

```text
annual funding transfer = R * f
```

A Payer holder with position value `p` has instantaneous notional `pL`, so their annualized funding transfer is:

```text
holder funding transfer = p * L * f
```

Positive `f` means Receiver pays Payer. Negative `f` means Payer pays Receiver. Funding is variable, can reverse, and is not guaranteed yield.

For the normalized curve and worked transfers, see [Funding and carry](/learn/funding-and-carry).

## Entering and exiting a position

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the market">
    Check the tracked price, collateral, oracle, funding-rate model, pool sizes, current funding, fees, and circuit-breaker behavior.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a pool">
    Mint into Receiver for the tracked return, or into Payer for the opposite return at current leverage `L = R / P`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive shares">
    Deposit collateral and receive ERC-4626 shares in that pool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor">
    Pool sizes, funding, fees, collateral value, and oracle settlement can all change your share value.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Burn shares">
    Redeem for your proportional claim after the market settles and applicable fees apply.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Pool shares are transferable, but that does not guarantee an active secondary market or a particular redemption value. If the market uses yield-bearing collateral, collateral yield can also move both pools. It is separate from funding and price PnL.

## Settlement

Settlement is interaction-driven. Deposits, mints, withdrawals, and redemptions settle the market before continuing. There is no separate public keeper action that updates every block.

During settlement, the market reconciles collateral, accrues fees, reads the oracle and funding-rate model, nets price PnL with funding, and updates each pool's collateral balance. Existing share counts do not change. The collateral value represented by each share does.

If an accepted oracle move exceeds the time-adjusted circuit-breaker threshold, the market skips that update's price transfer and records the new price as its next baseline. Funding can still settle. The skipped price PnL is not deferred.

## What the market fixes

Each market is uniquely identified by three dependency addresses:

```text
(collateral, oracle, funding-rate model)
```

Those dependencies and the two pool addresses are fixed for that market. A different tuple creates a different market.

The factory owner can enable funding-rate models for future markets, set capped fees, and collect accrued protocol fees. Review the current market and fee settings before entering.

<Warning>
Receiver and Payer can lose most of their pool value. The minimum-pool floor and circuit breaker constrain specific transfers; they do not guarantee fair pricing, liquidity, or capital protection.
</Warning>

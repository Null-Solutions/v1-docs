Null is a market primitive for creating onchain exposure to almost anything with a reliable price feed. It turns a total return swap into two transparent pools: one side receives the tracked asset's return, while the other side provides the collateral that backs it.

There is no loan, margin account, liquidation engine, or hidden counterparty. Every gain is paid from collateral already inside the market.

## The idea in one minute

Imagine a market tracking an asset such as ETH, gold, or an equity:

1. **Receiver** deposits collateral to receive the asset's price return.
2. **Payer** deposits collateral to back Receiver and takes the other side of that return.
3. An oracle reports how the asset's price has moved.
4. Null moves value between the two pools to reflect that movement.
5. Funding also moves between the pools to encourage enough Payer backing.

If the tracked price rises, value normally moves from Payer to Receiver. If it falls, value normally moves from Receiver to Payer. Nobody is borrowing the tracked asset, and the market never needs to liquidate an account.

<Info>
Users do not need to own the tracked asset. They deposit the market's collateral token and receive pool shares representing their position.
</Info>

## Receiver and Payer

### Receiver

Receiver is the straightforward side for taking the tracked asset's return. If the asset rises by 5%, the Receiver pool is credited based on that move. If it falls by 5%, the Receiver pool is debited.

Receiver is synthetic exposure, not ownership of the underlying asset. It does not provide voting rights, dividends, redemption rights, or any other benefit attached to owning that asset unless the market rules explicitly account for it.

### Payer

Payer supplies the reserve that makes Receiver fully collateralized. It takes the opposite price transfer and earns or pays funding according to how much Payer coverage the market has.

Payer's percentage return can be larger than the tracked asset's move because the same transfer is spread across the Payer pool. A smaller Payer pool means each Payer share absorbs more of the market's price movement.

## A simple example

Suppose a market has:

- 1,000 units in Receiver;
- 200 units in Payer; and
- a tracked price that rises by 2%.

The price transfer is 2% of the Receiver pool, or 20 units:

```text
Receiver: 1,000 → 1,020
Payer:      200 →   180
```

Receiver gains 2%. Payer loses 10% because the 20-unit transfer is measured against its smaller pool. A price decrease would move value in the other direction.

This example ignores funding, fees, collateral yield, and safety bounds, but it shows the core exchange.

## Why funding exists

Receiver exposure is only as strong as the collateral backing it. Null uses a funding-rate model to encourage the market toward a target amount of Payer coverage.

- When Payer backing is below the target, funding can flow from Receiver to Payer.
- When Payer backing is above the target, funding can reverse and flow from Payer to Receiver.

Funding is signed and automatic. It is not a fixed yield, and either side may pay it.

## Positions are pooled shares

Depositing into either side mints ERC-4626 pool shares. Those shares represent a percentage of the entire pool, not a private trade with its own entry price.

This makes positions:

- **fungible** — shares from the same pool are interchangeable;
- **composable** — wallets and other applications can hold standard tokenized shares;
- **liquid by design** — users enter and exit against pooled collateral rather than finding a bilateral counterparty; and
- **transparent** — share value comes from the pool's visible collateral and accounting state.

If the collateral itself is yield-bearing, both pools can also benefit from that collateral yield while the market tracks its separate price exposure.

## What makes Null different

### Fully collateralized

Null can only transfer value already held by the market. It cannot create debt or an undercollateralized receivable.

### No liquidations

There are no leveraged accounts to monitor and no liquidation auction. Losses appear directly in pool share value and are bounded by available pool collateral.

### Markets for any reliable oracle

A market can track crypto, equities, commodities, rates, or other measurable values without requiring custody of the tracked asset.

### Immutable market rules

Each market permanently fixes its collateral token, oracle, and funding-rate model when it is created. Participants can inspect the rules before depositing, and an administrator cannot silently replace them for an existing market.

### Permissionless, pooled infrastructure

Anyone can create a canonical market from Factory-enabled components. Standardized pools and routers make those markets easier for users and applications to discover and integrate.

## How a position moves through Null

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a market">
    Review the tracked asset, collateral, oracle, funding, fees, liquidity, and current pool coverage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Receiver or Payer">
    Receiver takes the tracked return. Payer supplies backing and takes the other side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mint shares">
    Deposit collateral into the selected pool and receive ERC-4626 shares.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the market settle">
    When the market updates, Null validates the oracle and nets price movement with funding before moving collateral between the pools.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Burn shares">
    Redeem pool shares for your proportional claim on the collateral currently available to that pool.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Safety boundaries

The protocol includes a minimum-pool floor and a price circuit breaker that can skip unusually large price moves during a single update. These controls limit specific settlement behavior; they do not guarantee a position's value or eliminate risk.

<Warning>
Receiver and Payer positions can lose value. Full collateralization does not remove oracle, smart-contract, liquidity, collateral, funding, or market risk.
</Warning>

Market makers supply Payer collateral to back Receiver exposure. Economically, Payer is short the tracked asset on Null: it generally loses when the tracked price rises and gains when it falls. To reduce that directional exposure, a market maker can hold Payer on Null and buy the tracked asset, or a close equivalent, elsewhere.

The two legs create a basis trade: **short on Null, long elsewhere**. The goal is to offset most price exposure and earn the remaining carry after hedge costs.

<Warning>
Market making is not risk-free or guaranteed to be profitable. It adds external-venue, margin, execution, basis, funding, oracle, smart-contract, collateral, and liquidity risks.
</Warning>

## The two legs

A hedged Payer position has two coordinated legs:

1. **Null Payer:** supplies backing, receives or pays Null funding, and shares in collateral yield.
2. **External long:** offsets Payer's synthetic short exposure to the tracked asset.

The external venue needs a market that closely tracks the same asset. Null's reference hedger currently uses Hyperliquid perpetuals, but the hedge formula is venue-independent.

## Size the hedge

Let:

- `p` be the current value of your Payer position;
- `R` be total Receiver pool assets;
- `P` be total Payer pool assets; and
- `S` be the tracked asset's oracle price.

`R`, `P`, and `p` come from the current onchain pool state and your Payer share ownership. `S` comes from the market's configured oracle. A Payer is a negative-size position in the Receiver pool, with the market's collateral held inside that structure. The short scale relative to Payer capital is:

```text
R / P
```

That ratio is determined by the current pool balances. It is not a trader-selected setting and changes as either pool changes.

The required external long is:

```text
h = p * (R / P) / S tracked-asset units
H = h * S = p * (R / P)             external notional
```

Equivalently, if you own the fraction `p / P` of the Payer pool, you hedge that fraction of Receiver's total asset exposure `R / S`.

### Example

Suppose:

```text
Payer position p = $7.50
Receiver pool R  = $1,050
Payer pool P     = $150
Oracle price S   = $105
```

Then:

```text
R / P = 1,050 / 150 = 7
h = 7.50 * 7 / 105 = 0.50 units
H = 0.50 * 105 = $52.50
```

The market maker holds the Null Payer shares and opens a long of `0.50` tracked-asset units externally.

## Why price moves do not normally trigger a rebalance

During a normal price settlement, the oracle price and Receiver assets move together while your Payer position moves in the opposite direction. These changes offset each other in the hedge formula, leaving `h` unchanged.

The hedger should not chase every price move. Recompute from fresh state when flows, funding, or your Payer holdings change the position's actual exposure.

## When to recompute

For a small change, the hedge sensitivity is:

```text
dh / h = dp / p - dP / P + dR / R - dS / S
```

Protocol events simplify that expression:

| Event | Relative target change | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full price settlement | `0` | None |
| Receiver flow `ΔR` | `ΔR / R` | Follow the flow |
| Other-Payer flow `ΔP` | `-ΔP / P` | Trade against the flow |
| Funding over `Δt` | `-f * Δt / 365 days` | Slow drift; direction follows funding's sign |
| Your Payer mint or burn | Recompute from the new `p` | Resize the hedge |

For a large deposit by other Payers, use the exact target multiplier `P / (P + ΔP)` rather than the small-flow approximation.

Circuit-breaker events, solvency-floor truncation, and stale market state can break the assumptions used for normal price settlement. In these cases, suspend automated resizing and reassess both legs.

The reference hedger recomputes the target from fresh pool state instead of accumulating estimated deltas.

## Use a no-trade band

Trading every tiny change creates unnecessary fees and slippage. Define a tolerance `tol` around the target:

```text
lower bound = h * (1 - tol)
upper bound = h * (1 + tol)
```

Only rebalance when the external position leaves that band. The reference implementation uses a default `0.5%` tolerance and waits when the required adjustment is below the venue's minimum order size.

## Evaluate expected carry

Hedging can reduce directional price exposure, but it does not guarantee a profitable position. Estimate the carry from both legs before opening the trade.

Let:

- `p` be your current Payer position value;
- `R / P` be the Payer short scale (negative Receiver size vs Payer capital);
- `H = p × R / P` be the external hedge notional;
- `f` be Null funding APR paid by Receivers to Payers from collateral yield (product model: `f ≈ s * y`, never negative for the Null leg);
- `f_ext` be external funding, where positive means the external long pays; and
- `y` be the collateral yield rate on Payer capital.

```text
Null funding       =  H * f
Collateral yield   =  p * y
External funding   = -H * f_ext

Net carry          =  p * [y + (R / P) * (f - f_ext)]
```

Null funding applies to exposure notional `H = p × R / P` because funding is charged on Receiver notional and spreads across Payer. Collateral yield applies to the Payer position value `p`. See [Funding and carry](/learn/funding-and-carry).

### Carry example

Assume:

```text
Payer position p         = $100
Short scale R / P        = 5
Null funding f           = 1.5%
External long funding    = 1%
Collateral yield y       = 5%
```

The hedge notional is `$500`. Projected annual carry is:

```text
Null funding       = $500 * 1.5% =  $7.50
Collateral yield   = $100 * 5%   =  $5.00
External funding   = $500 * 1%   = -$5.00
Net carry                            $7.50
```

If external funding rises to `3%`, projected annual net carry becomes `-$2.50`. A larger short scale magnifies a negative funding spread just as readily as a positive one.

### Screen the opportunity

When estimating projected carry, account for entry and exit fees, rebalancing costs, slippage, gas, external margin opportunity cost, and potential basis losses.

Review:

- current and historical Null funding;
- current and predicted external funding;
- Receiver/Payer coverage and how quickly it is changing;
- collateral yield quality and withdrawal behavior;
- oracle and external-market trading hours;
- Payer liquidity and venue depth; and
- the external equity needed to maintain a conservative liquidation buffer.

Do not open solely because one funding snapshot looks attractive. Vault yield and external funding both move.

## Understand basis PnL

The Null leg settles against its oracle, while the external long is marked and filled at the venue price. Those prices can diverge. Fees, slippage, partial fills, downtime, and oracle-versus-venue drift can leave the two legs mismatched.

```text
Null PnL = current Payer value - opening value - deposits and withdrawals

Hedge PnL = change in unrealized PnL
          + realized PnL
          + external funding
          - venue fees

Net PnL = Null PnL + Hedge PnL
```

A position can have attractive projected carry and still lose money through basis, execution, funding reversal, collateral behavior, or liquidation of the external hedge.

## Operating lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a market">
    Confirm that the tracked asset has a suitable external hedge venue. Review Payer coverage, Null funding, collateral yield, liquidity, fees, oracle hours, and the external funding rate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mint Payer shares">
    Deposit collateral into the Payer pool. Record the shares received and the position value used to establish your PnL baseline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the external long">
    Calculate `h = p(R/P)/S` and buy that many tracked-asset units on the hedge venue. Use enough venue equity to maintain a conservative liquidation buffer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor both legs">
    Track fresh Null pool state, oracle and venue prices, funding on both venues, collateral yield, margin, fills, fees, and the hedge deviation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebalance only when required">
    Trade the difference between current and target hedge after the tolerance, lot-size, minimum-notional, margin, and order-cap checks pass.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close both legs">
    Burn the Payer shares and close the remaining external long as one coordinated exit. Confirm final fills and funding before treating the strategy as settled.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reference hedger behavior

Null's reference hedger is an operator tool, not part of the protocol:

- it reads live, simulated market state from the indexer on every new block;
- it verifies the wallet's Payer ownership onchain;
- it opens an offsetting Hyperliquid long;
- it suspends trading when Null state is stale;
- it uses a tolerance band, venue minimum, lot precision, margin gate, and per-order notional cap;
- it defaults to dry-run mode; and
- it records venue funding, fills, fees, and PnL from Hyperliquid's ledger rather than estimating them.

The reference hedger never opens or closes the Null Payer position. The operator controls that leg and remains responsible for capital, keys, venue setup, monitoring, and emergency procedures.

Morpho Blue needs a price for Receiver pool shares before it will lend against them, and no external feed quotes that asset. The two oracles on this page manufacture the price from the Null pool itself. Both implement Morpho's `price()` interface at `1e36` scale, and both answer the same underlying question from opposite sides of the two Margin books: what is a Receiver share worth, right now, in the units the book lends?

Neither oracle reads Chainlink. The Chainlink oracle on the Null market prices the tracked asset for spot settlement. These oracles price the pool share for Morpho, and they derive that price from the pool's own conversion functions.

## The long oracle

`NullV1MorphoOracle` serves the long book, where Morpho lends USDC against Receiver shares. It therefore quotes loan assets per Receiver share:

```text
collateralAssets = receiverPool.previewRedeem(sampleShares)
loanAssets = vault.previewRedeem(collateralAssets)   // or passthrough when collateral is not a vault
price = loanAssets * 1e36 / sampleShares
```

The important choice here is `previewRedeem`. Morpho is being promised the USDC a redemption would actually raise if the collateral had to be sold, exit fee included, not a theoretical mid price. When the market collateral is a vault such as `nmUSDC`, the quote takes one extra step through the vault so the final number lands in loan assets. When the collateral is not a vault, that step is a passthrough.

## The inverse oracle

`NullV1MorphoInverseOracle` serves the short book, where Morpho lends Receiver shares against `nmUSDC`. The units flip. This oracle quotes Receiver shares per unit of collateral:

```text
price = receiverPool.previewDeposit(sampleAssets) * 1e36 / sampleAssets
```

Here `previewDeposit` is the honest quote, entry fee included, because it states how many shares a deposit of collateral actually mints. The price rises as Receiver shares become more expensive in collateral terms, which is exactly the direction that should tighten a short position's health.

## Which oracle belongs to which book

Pairing the wrong oracle with a book is not a cosmetic mistake. The two books lend different tokens, so their prices are stated in different units, and a swapped oracle quotes a number Morpho will happily use in the wrong denomination.

| Book | Morpho lends | Oracle | Price states |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long | USDC | `NullV1MorphoOracle` | Loan assets per Receiver share |
| Short | Receiver shares | `NullV1MorphoInverseOracle` | Receiver shares per collateral unit |

When you create or verify a book, check the oracle field of `marketParams` against this table before you check anything else. It is the fastest way to catch a misconfigured market, and it costs one read.

## Why the price can lag

Both oracles read the pool's last settled state. They do not settle the market themselves, and they do not push a Chainlink report into it. The price Morpho sees only moves when some interaction settles the Null market and writes a new conversion rate.

In practice this means a borrow or a liquidation can be priced one interaction behind the market's Chainlink oracle. If you are about to rely on the Morpho price for size, either settle the market first in the same flow, which the router's `updateData` argument exists to do, or accept the settled price as the price you are trading against. This lag is a property of the design, not a stale feed you can fix by resubmitting.

You can lose some or all of what you put in. The two-pool design explains where gains and losses go. It does not soften the market or promise that a safeguard will work the way you expect. This page covers the main risks. It is not a complete list.

## Risks by product

| Risk | Who it affects | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The price moves against you | Receivers and Payers | Receiver value follows the price down. Payer value moves the other way, scaled by `R / P`, so a thin Payer pool can lose fast when the price rises. |
| You can't leave when you want | Payers | A **Payer sell** cannot leave the Payer pool smaller than a fifth of the Receiver pool. At that line, or after a rise pushes past it, Payers wait until Receivers sell or new Payers join. See [Pool ratio cap](/protocol/pool-ratio-cap). |
| You don't own the asset | Everyone holding a synthetic | No votes, no dividends, no investor protections. You hold a claim on collateral, not the stock or coin. |
| Interest disappoints | Receivers and Payers | Funding depends on the Collateral Vault actually earning. It can be small for long periods, or pause while the vault recovers. |
| The collateral falls | Everyone | If the Collateral Vault loses value, the collateral behind every pool falls with it. |
| Liquidation | Margin | If your loan-to-value reaches the 96.5% LLTV, Morpho can sell your collateral. You may get back little or nothing. The **Liquidation price** shown is an estimate. |
| Closing a short can wait | Margin | Opening a long and closing a short both buy into the Receiver pool. While the pools are at the ratio cap, both are blocked. A short's interest keeps running in the meantime, and it can still be liquidated. |
| Liquidity | Savings and Lending | Lent money can't be withdrawn until borrowers repay. Large withdrawals may wait or come in parts. |
| Bad debt | Savings and Lending | If a borrower is liquidated for less than the debt, lenders can lose principal. |
| Hedges | Payers | A hedge on another venue has its own funding, fees and margin calls. It can cost more than Null pays. |

## Prices and settlement

Every market depends on a Chainlink price feed. A feed can go stale, report a price you did not expect, or be unavailable when you want to trade. Markets settle when someone deposits or withdraws, so a preview can change by the time your transaction confirms.

If one settlement would move the price more than 20%, the price move is skipped and the new price becomes the starting point. That protects the pools from a bad print or a split, but a real move of that size is lost to whoever was positioned for it. See [Circuit breaker](/protocol/circuit-breaker).

## Contracts and settings

Null is software, and software can have bugs. A market's collateral, price feed and funding model never change after it is created. The factory owner can still enable new funding models and adjust fees within the published caps.

The core contracts were audited by Nethermind in August 2026. Some newer contracts, including the funding model now in use and the Morpho router, came later and are outside that review. Read [Audits](/protocol/audits) for the exact scope. An audit reduces risk. It does not remove it.

## Risks that are yours alone

You control your wallet and everything you sign. A wrong address, a compromised device or an approval you didn't mean can cause a loss Null cannot reverse. Tax treatment depends on where you live and can change.

Null is not offered in the United Kingdom and is unavailable where using it would break the law or sanctions. The app cannot decide your eligibility for you. Read the [Legal notices](/legal-notices) before you trade.

If any of this leaves you unsure, the sensible trade is usually the one you postpone.

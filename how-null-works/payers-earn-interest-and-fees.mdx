Both pools hold nUSDC, and nUSDC earns interest in the [Collateral Vault](/how-null-works/usdc-becomes-nusdc). Receivers hand their share of that interest to Payers. That is what following the price costs a Receiver, and what a Payer is paid for taking the opposite side.

The app calls this transfer **funding**. On Null it only ever flows one way, from Receivers to Payers, because its source is interest that has already been earned.

## A worked example

Take the $100 Receiver and $50 Payer from [Two pools](/how-null-works/nusdc-backs-two-pools), with the vault earning 5% a year. The Receiver's interest goes to the Payer, on top of the Payer's own.

| | Exposure | Deposit | Interest kept, at 5% a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Receiver | $100 | $100 | $0. It goes to the Payer. |
| Payer | $100, the other side | $50 | $7.50, which is 7.5% of the exposure it backs |

The Payer keeps the interest on both deposits: $2.50 on its own $50 and $5 from the Receiver's $100. Measured against the $50 it put in, that is 15% a year before any price move.

This is an example only. Rates change, Payers share the interest between them, and price moves and fees are not included.

## Fees go to Payers too

Every time a Receiver opens or closes a position, the market charges a small fee, and that fee goes to the Payer pool, not to Null. It sits on top of the interest, and it adds up with volume. In the example below, a $10,000 Receiver pool trades $200,000 a year. At 0.1% each way, that pays Payers $200, lifting them from $750 of interest to $950, or 19% on $5,000.

| What Payers earn | Where it comes from | How it grows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Their own interest | The Collateral Vault, on the Payer deposit. | With the vault rate. |
| Receivers' interest | The Collateral Vault, on the Receiver deposits. | With the vault rate and `R / P`. |
| Receiver fees | The opening and closing fee on every Receiver trade. | With how often Receivers trade. |

It works the other way too: when a Payer opens or closes, that fee goes to the Receiver pool. See [What it costs](/how-null-works/what-it-costs) for rates and caps.

## Why this beats paying funding

On a perpetual future, you pay funding on your whole position, and the rate can swing against you. On Null, a Receiver pays nothing while holding. You give up the interest your own deposit would have earned, and pay a small fee only when you open or close.

## The rule for Payers

For a Payer, the interest works out at roughly:

```text
carry ≈ y × (1 + R / P)
```

`y` is the vault's interest rate. The `1` is your own deposit earning. The `R / P` part is the Receivers' interest, spread across the Payer pool. When Receivers outnumber Payers, each Payer earns more, and carries more price risk. [Payers](/payers) goes deeper.

## The rates in the app

| Rate | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Collateral APR** | What the Null Collateral Vault is earning right now. |
| **Funding APR** | That interest, as paid from the Receiver pool to Payers. |
| **Net APR** | The total for the side you hold. For a Payer it is scaled by `R / P`. It describes current rates, not a promise. |
| **AER** | The Savings rate. It comes from the Null Savings Vault lending into long markets, not from funding. |

## Rules that keep it simple

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Only real interest | Funding pays out interest the vault has actually earned, measured against its previous high. |
| Never negative | If the vault has a bad stretch, funding pauses until it recovers. Payers never pay Receivers. |
| Capped | A single interval cannot pass across more than a 100% annual rate. Anything above stays with Receivers. |
| Tiny pools skip | If either pool is at its minimum size, that interval's interest is not charged later. |

Never negative describes the transfer, not your position. If the vault loses value, the collateral in both pools falls with it.

## What funding is not

Funding is not a fee. It stays inside the market and moves between its two pools. Null does not take it as revenue. Entry and exit fees are separate, see [Fees](/how-null-works/what-it-costs).

If you hedge a Payer position somewhere else, that hedge has its own funding, fees and margin. Judge the two together. The contract detail is in [Funding rate model](/protocol/funding-rate-model).

There is no deposit account to top up before you can use Null. **Cash** is
the USDC already sitting in the wallet you connected, shown in USD in your
portfolio. When a product needs money, it spends from that wallet balance.
When you withdraw from Savings or sell on Spot, the USDC comes back to the
same wallet. Your account, in other words, is your wallet.

This is easy to misread if you are used to exchanges, where cash usually
means money held on the platform. Here it does not. Null never takes custody
of the USDC in your wallet, and nothing about holding Cash earns a return.
Cash becomes productive only when you choose to put it to work.

## How each product uses Cash

Every product starts from the same wallet, but each one does something
different with what it takes. Savings asks you to **Add money**, which
deposits USDC into the Null Savings Vault. Spot uses **Buy** to wrap USDC
into `nmUSDC` and deposit that collateral into a pool. Lending supplies
USDC, or in some books a Receiver share, to a Morpho book when you choose
**Lend**. Margin takes USDC as the equity behind a **Long** or **Short**.

One cost sits outside all four products, and it catches people out. Every
transaction needs ETH for gas. A wallet full of USDC and empty of ETH can
see its balance perfectly well and still be unable to do anything with it,
so treat a small ETH buffer as part of funding, not as an afterthought.

| What you are doing | What you need | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Savings | USDC | Into the Null Savings Vault through **Add money** |
| Spot | USDC | Wrapped to `nmUSDC` and deposited into a pool through **Buy** |
| Lending | USDC or a Receiver share | Supplied to a Morpho book through **Lend** |
| Margin | USDC | Posted as equity for a **Long** or **Short** |
| Any transaction | ETH | Paid as gas on Ethereum mainnet |

## Getting USDC to your wallet

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy your address from Receive">
    Open **Receive** and copy the address of your connected wallet. Read the
    chain notice before you send anything, because the network matters as
    much as the address itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send USDC on Ethereum">
    Send USDC from an exchange, a bridge, or another wallet, and send it on
    Ethereum. USDC arriving on another chain will not appear as Cash, even
    though it reached a wallet you control.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Leave ETH behind for gas">
    Keep a buffer of ETH rather than spending the wallet down to zero. Your
    next deposit, sale, or withdrawal will need it, and a transaction you
    cannot afford to confirm is a position you cannot manage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm Cash, then choose a product">
    Back on Home, your USDC should show under **Cash**. From there, **Invest**
    lets you pick Savings, Spot, Lending, or Margin, depending on what you
    want the money to do.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Returns work in the other direction through the same wallet. Savings
withdrawals and Spot sells send USDC back to you, subject to the liquidity
available at the time and any fees that apply. If a balance looks smaller
than you expected after a withdrawal, check what was actually free to leave
before assuming something went wrong.

Signing in means connecting a wallet you already control, on Ethereum mainnet. That wallet holds your positions, signs your transactions and receives anything you withdraw or sell. Null never holds your funds.

That has three consequences. If you lose access to your wallet, Null cannot restore it. If you send assets on the wrong network, they won't appear. If you connect a different wallet, you'll see a different portfolio.

## What you need

| | |
| --- | --- |
| A wallet | One you control and can sign with. |
| USDC | On Ethereum. It is the cash every product uses. |
| A little ETH | For gas. Every transaction on Ethereum costs some. |
| Eligibility | Null is not offered in the United Kingdom, and the app blocks sanctioned regions. See [Legal notices](/legal). |

The trade ticket says **By trading you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy**. If those notices and an agreement shown in the app ever differ, the agreement controls.

## Connect

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the connection">
    Choose **Sign in** and pick your wallet. Approving links the address to your session. It doesn't move any funds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check you're on Ethereum">
    If your wallet asks you to sign on another network, stop and check. Null positions live on Ethereum mainnet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the address">
    The connected address is where positions are held and proceeds return. Make sure it's the wallet you mean to use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See your Cash">
    USDC in that wallet shows as **Cash**. [Fund your account](/get-started/fund-your-account) covers getting it there.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Receiving assets

**Receive**, on Spot and among the Home actions, shows your wallet address so you or an exchange can send to it. It doesn't open a position or commit you to anything.

<Warning>
Null cannot recover a wallet, reverse a confirmed transaction or move funds without your signature. Check the network and the address before you send.
</Warning>

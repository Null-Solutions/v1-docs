Every Null position can lose value. Full collateralization bounds what the market can transfer; it does not remove market, oracle, or smart-contract risk. There is **no liquidation** — your maximum loss in a pool is your share of that pool's assets.

## Risk categories

<Accordion title="Directional risk — Receiver">
  Receiver gains when the tracked price rises and loses when it falls. Settlement losses are bounded by settleable Receiver assets above `MIN_POOL_ASSETS`.

  Funding is signed: Receiver pays Payer when coverage is below the FRM target, and receives funding when coverage is above it. Default zero crossing is `P = 0.5R`.

  Higher risk with high index volatility, under-target Payer coverage (funding cost), and sustained downside trends.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reserve risk — Payer">
  Payer loses when the tracked price rises and gains when it falls. Moves inside the circuit-breaker bound settle in full — a 7% rise every hour can erode a thin Payer pool quickly.

  The breaker skips oversized single updates; it does not clamp them. Thin coverage can mean high funding income and a narrow price buffer.

  Higher risk with sustained upside, small Payer relative to Receiver, and repeated breaker resets from a higher baseline.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Oracle risk">
  Ordinary stale periods revert price reads and block settlement-dependent actions. After a prolonged stale period (~10 days in current Pyth/Chainlink wrappers), the oracle may return the last stored price so users are not permanently locked.

  Escape restores liveness, not fair value — you bear basis risk between the last oracle price and true market value. See [Oracles](/markets/oracles).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Smart contract risk">
  Protective patterns include duplicate-market prevention, `onlyPools` market accounting, router deadlines/slippage, and reentrancy guards. Treat the protocol as experimental: audits (when available) do not guarantee absence of bugs. Core logic is in Factory, Market, Pool, and SettlementLib; routers are periphery.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Collateral risk">
  Markets assume standard ERC-20 behavior (exact transfer amounts). Fee-on-transfer is unsupported. Pauses, blacklists, rebases, or external vault losses still matter. Reconciliation aligns accounting with balances; it does not remove collateral risk. See [Collateral](/markets/collateral).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Liquidity / thin markets">
  Thin Payer coverage raises funding toward the FRM bound while shrinking the buffer against price rises. Thin Payer also caps Receiver upside from price gains. There is no liquidity backstop — redemptions pay whatever the pool currently holds.
</Accordion>

## Summary

You cannot be liquidated by another participant. If your pool loses value through price, funding, fees, or collateral drawdown, your share falls with it. Evaluate collateral, oracle, FRM, pool sizes, funding, fees, and circuit-breaker behavior before depositing — [Risks and parameters](/learn/risks-and-parameters).
